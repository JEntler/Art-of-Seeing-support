The guide

A moment to begin.

A few simple controls make the rhythm yours.

Start here.

  1. Open The Art of Seeing, then click its eye in the Mac menu bar.
  2. Choose Start Reminders. The default is 20 minutes of work followed by a 20-second Distance Look timer when you start the break.
  3. Use Enable Notifications if you want native banners. You can keep using the menu-bar indicator without granting notification permission.

Opening the menu does not reset the timer. Launch at Login and starting reminders on launch are separate, off-by-default choices in Settings.

What each control does.

Reset Timer
Starts a fresh work interval and clears the current reminder. It keeps a manual pause and never records a finished break.
Pause / Resume
Pause recurring reminders for a chosen time or indefinitely. Resume starts a fresh interval when your schedule permits.
Take a Break Now
Starts a guided routine by your choice, including while paused. The previous reminder intention is restored afterward.
Snooze / Skip
Snooze delays the current opportunity; Skip closes it and starts a fresh interval. Neither counts as a finished break.
Finish Early
Stops a running routine. Optional history records it as partial.
I'm Back
With the optional wait-for-return setting, starts the next work interval after a break.
Quit
Ends the app and cancels its owned reminders. No background helper keeps reminding you.

A due reminder remains actionable for five minutes. If unanswered, the cycle continues without assuming you took a break. After a long absence, the app starts fresh instead of presenting a backlog.

Make room in your day.

Work hours are optional. Choose weekdays and a start/end window, including overnight windows. Quiet hours take precedence. A manual pause stays paused through schedule changes, sleep, and relaunch.

Until Tomorrow uses 9:00 AM on the next local day without a schedule, or the next eligible start on or after tomorrow with a schedule. The app shows the actual return time.

Display sleep and documented session changes suspend reminders. A short observed absence preserves the remaining interval; an absence of two minutes or more starts a fresh interval. A break interrupted by an unavailable session is not recorded as finished.

These signals cannot verify that you looked away, and independent screen-lock detection is limited by public macOS APIs.

Gentle prompts, no performance test.

Read the instruction before looking away. Every activity works without watching an animation.

Distance Look

Look away from your screen at something comfortably distant—about 20 ft / 6 m away when practical. Let your eyes rest; don't strain for detail.

Gentle Blinks

Blink slowly and completely a few times. Keep it gentle.

Eyes Closed

Let your eyes rest gently closed.

Look Around

Let your attention move naturally around your surroundings. No staring or forcing detail.

Step Away

Step away from the screen for a few minutes when it's safe and practical.

Optional activity; not part of the default routine.

Change Viewing Distance

Look comfortably at a nearby object, then something farther away. Keep it easy and stop if uncomfortable.

Optional activity; not part of the default routine.

No distant view? Gently close your eyes for this break. An image on your monitor is not a distant view.

Your record, if useful.

Optional local history records app activity, not where you looked or how your eyes felt.

History is off by default. When enabled, it distinguishes started and finished timers, partial or interrupted routines, skipped reminders, and unanswered opportunities. It is capped at 90 days. Turn off Record local history and choose Save Changes to stop new recording. Choose Delete All History… and confirm Delete History to remove existing records.

Keep it comfortable and safe.

A screen-break and habit-support tool, not an eyesight test or medical treatment. It does not measure or correct vision.

Seek professional care for persistent vision changes. Seek urgent eye care for sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, a sudden increase in floaters or flashes, or a curtain or shadow across your vision.

Never look directly at the sun or an intense light during a break.

Do activities only when safe. Stop if you feel pain, discomfort, dizziness, or double vision. Keep prescribed correction as directed by your clinician. Do not press on your eyes or force detail through blur.

What these reminders can tell you.

The app schedules reminders and guided timers. It does not observe your gaze or measure clinical outcomes.

20–20–20 is a familiar reminder pattern, not a proven universal dose. Research findings differ, and this app has not been clinically tested.

Research into scheduled breaks has used different methods and found different results. A study of another tool cannot establish a benefit for this app. See the 2023 study of 20–20–20 reminders and 2023 experiment on break timing. General eye-care information is available from the National Eye Institute.
